How to Quote a Kitchen Respray

Price the surface, allow for respray prep, and show the customer what they’re paying for.

By Colin Bedford, Paintworkz — a 2 pac spray shop in Hamilton, Victoria · Updated November 2026

Kitchen respraying is a big business in the UK: homeowners would rather pay to respray solid doors than rip out a kitchen. But quoting it is tricky. Two kitchens with the same number of doors can be completely different jobs, and an under-priced respray loses money fast.

Here's how spray shops can build a respray quote that covers the real work.

Price the surface, not just the door count

Many respray firms quote per door or per unit, because customers understand it. That works if you know what a "door" is. A 715 mm base door and a 2000 mm larder door are not the same job. Measure each part in millimetres and work out its area in m², then decide how to present it:

  • Per m² — fairest, and scales with paint and labour.
  • Per door, with size bands — standard, tall/larder, drawer front, end panel.
  • Per linear metre — plinths, cornice, pelmet and fillers.

Our pricing guide walks through all three with a worked example.

Resprays need more prep than new MDF

A respray isn't new work. Existing doors come with grease, old lacquer, chips and the occasional previous DIY paint job. Allow for:

  • Degreasing and keying every surface, especially around handles and on base units near the hob.
  • Repairs — filling chips and dents, re-gluing loose edging.
  • Adhesion — foil-wrap (vinyl) doors and some laminates need specialist primers or aren't suitable at all. Check before quoting.
  • Profiles — shaker and in-frame doors take far longer to sand than slab doors.

On-site or in the workshop?

Some firms spray doors in a workshop and paint carcasses on site; others take everything away. Each way has its own costs: masking a customer's kitchen, travel time, removing and refitting hinges, and adjusting doors on refit. Put them on the quote as separate lines so the customer can see what they're paying for.

Colour, finish and VAT

  • Colour matching — matching a sample or a manufacturer's colour takes time and test sprays. Charge per colour.
  • Two-tone kitchens — a different colour on the island or wall units means two mixes, two gun cleans and more masking.
  • Finish — matt and satin are forgiving; high gloss shows every flaw and needs more cutting back.
  • VAT — if you're VAT registered, be clear whether your price includes 20% VAT. Homeowners compare totals; trade customers compare net.

A shaker kitchen, satin, one colour. Rates are made-up examples to show the maths — use your own.

Know your cost per m²

Add up a month of paint, primer, hardener, thinners and abrasives, then wages and overheads, and divide each by the m² you sprayed. That's your break-even per m². Your respray rates need to sit well above it, because resprays carry more prep than new work.
